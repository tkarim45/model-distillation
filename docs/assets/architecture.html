Model Distillation — Architecture

Distill Claude's topic-classification behaviour into a local TF-IDF + logistic-regression student, then measure the three numbers that decide whether distillation is worth it: how much quality survives, what an imperfect teacher costs, and when the student pays for itself.

A real task with real gold labels

  • • a 4-category slice of 20 Newsgroups with headers, footers and quotes stripped, so it is genuine content classification
  • • the teacher labels each document zero-shot; the student learns ONLY from the teacher's labels; both are scored against held-out gold
  • • teacher labels are cached to a committed JSON file, so the exact numbers reproduce for free and deleting the cache re-pays for the real Claude calls
  • • cost is computed from measured token usage, not estimated: 90.8k in / 1.9k out for $0.10

The gold-trained student is what makes the result readable

  • • training the SAME student on gold labels gives the capacity ceiling, which is the control the headline number needs
  • • teacher 0.907, distilled student 0.733, gold-trained ceiling 0.787, and the teacher's own label accuracy on the train pool is 0.933

Three findings

  • • the cheap student recovered 81% of Claude's accuracy for a one-time $0.10, and break-even is about 300 documents: label that many, and every query after is free
  • • most of the gap is the student's CAPACITY, not the teacher's noise: the 0.174 shortfall decomposes into 0.054 of distillation tax and about 0.12 of student simplicity, and only the second is worth fixing
  • • the student did not beat its teacher here, because denoising wins only when teacher noise rather than student capacity is the binding constraint
  • • the learning curve was still rising at 300 examples, so the run is label-limited rather than saturated
